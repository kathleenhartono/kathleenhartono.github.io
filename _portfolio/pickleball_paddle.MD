## Completed and Usable Paddle 
This pickleball paddle was designed to comply with the 2026 USA Pickleball Official Rulebook while maintaining durability to be used in a real game. The overall dimensions do not exceed the maximum allowable combined length and width of 24 inches. Note that in the gallery, there is a GIF of the paddle being able to withstand a ball. 

The paddle features a solid faced structure with a 15% honeycomb infill pattern. This infill was selected to reduce overall weight while still maintaining sufficient structural integrity and impact resistance. The honeycomb geometry distributes stress efficiently and provides a balance between stiffness and energy absorption for when used to hit the ball. For the paddle face, I used carbon fiber–reinforced ABS, which offers increased stiffness, durability, and resistance to repeated impacts. The handle core and structural support components were printed in PLA for rigidity and dimensional accuracy. 

Additionally, fillets and rounded edges were incorporated to reduce stress concentrations and improve durability for when the paddle could make contact with the ground. Since the paddle was able to connect internally, the outer heatsets designed to connect between the handle and paddle were not implement in the end print. 


## TPU Grip
The handle was designed as a multi-material system to balance structural strength with user comfort and performance during games. The handle was printed using PLA, which allows the handle to be rigid and stable while also ensuring a secure connection between the paddle face and the player. While PLA offers strength and dimensional accuracy, it can feel stiff and transmit vibration, so a TPU grip was added to improve handling. TPU was chosen for its flexibility, durability, allowing for a more secure and comfortable grip.

The grip incorporates a striped pattern that enhances comfort without significantly increasing the overall thickness of the handle. This pattern also reduces the rigid PLA and helping to minimize hand fatigue during extended play. The spacing within the pattern allows airflow between the hand and the grip, which reduces sweat buildup. This helps with having a consistent grip and improves control, especially during longer or more intense games.


## Interchangeable Edge Guard
The paddle was designed to include an interchangeable edge guard, intended as a component that attaches securely around the paddle perimeter. The design uses two heat-set inserts along the edge, allowing the guard to fasten to both the handle and paddle body for a stable and reinforced connection. This system was created to ensure the edge guard remains firmly in place during playing while still being removable when needed. However, due to print quality issues, this component was not successfully fabricated in the final build.

But, having this component would allow for easy replacement when wear happens from repeated ground contact, reducing long-term damage to the paddle itself. The edge guard was initially intended to be printed in PLA for rigidity and ease of production, but future iterations could use PETG or TPU to improve impact resistance and durability. This feature would extend the overall lifespan of the paddle and provide opportunities for customization based on playing conditions.

## Weight and Mass Balance
This paddle was intentionally designed within the heavy weight category (>8.3 oz) to prioritize power and shot stability over maximum maneuverability. A heavier paddle increases swing momentum, allowing for stronger drives and more powerful serves with less required swing speed. This can help reduce player effort over time by allowing the paddle to generate more force with each swing, allowing them to play longer.

To achieve this difference in weight, carbon fiber–reinforced ABS was used in the paddle to add both strength and mass, while internal infill distribution was distributing the weight throughout the paddle. The mass balance plays a critical role in determining swingweight, or how heavy the paddle feels during motion. By shifting more mass away from the handle and toward the head, the paddle experiences greater resistance when swung, increasing swingweight. This results in more powerful and stable shots due to higher momentum. However, this can reduce maneuverability, making quick reactions more difficult. To compensate, the handle was kept lighter using a PLA core with a TPU grip, helping maintain control and responsiveness.
